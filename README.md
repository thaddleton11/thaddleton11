## Reading material
I've spent a bit of time exploring the second hand book market on eBay and found a selection of cheap and interesting development focused books to read. I know pretty much any AI platform would be able to summaries the key points in just a few paragraphs but where's the fun in it! Despite some of them being 'old' now, it has been surprising how relevant they still are.

<details>
  <summary>Don't make me think - Sean Krug</summary>
  A UX book that suggests users shouldn't have the 'think' when interacting with a web application. Most user know what they want then they visit a page. The book argues that if they can't find it within 0.5 seconds then you've make them think, highlighting that they typically scan a page for what they want or, at least, want to navigate somewhere close. Therefore, by eliminating needless words, using predictable design and clear, visible
hierarchies, user retention and satisfaction significantly improves. It's also a strong advocate for early usability testing, something that pops up again and something I've had great success doing. Being released 20 years ago, seeing images of early and mid 2000s was... sobering.</details>

<details>
  <summary>The DevOps Handbook - Gene Kim</summary>
  In becoming a Technical Lead, I introduced and, in turn, became fully responsible for owning the DevOps and DevSecOps of the company. I bought this book to re-enforce my approach to DevOps, like our CI/CD pipelines, automated testing and observations. Perhaps aimed at much larger organisation structures it was, essentially, a reassuring read. Due to the nature of clients I have dealt with, a lot of the processes are required by them and closely related to NCSC guidance (their Software Security Code of Practice, for example). Still very relevant 10 years on.
</details>

<details>
  <summary>The Lean Startup - Eric Ries</summary>
Probably the most interesting book so far. In trying to further my understand of product development (not the coding type), my attention was drawn to this book. It encourages rapid experimentation and to launch MVPs as quickly as possible to start a "Build-Measure-Learn" feedback loop. It taking Lean project management ideas and applying them to business, not just engineering that I am somewhat familiar with. It highlight some great examples, many of which I introduced as a Lead Tech.
</details>
